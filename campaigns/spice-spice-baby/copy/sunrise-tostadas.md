# Sunrise Tostadas, approved copy

Rewritten 2026-09-10. The prior CMS description spent its only sentence on build
counts ("two slices of fresh avocado and four slices of fresh jalapeño on every
one"), which is recipe-card language, and buried the verde crema as a "drizzle."
These versions keep every ingredient but lead with the reason to order it.

**Menu line (for the CMS Ingredients field and Toast):**

> Three crisp corn tostadas loaded with slow-cooked carnitas, cheddar jack,
> salsa verde, lettuce and pico. Fresh avocado and jalapeño on every one, then a
> verde crema drizzle so the heat and the cool land in the same bite.

Shorter alternate, if the menu is tight:

> Three crisp corn tostadas, carnitas, cheddar jack, salsa verde, lettuce, pico,
> fresh avocado, jalapeño, verde crema drizzle.

**Press and email, used in the media fact sheet:**

> Three crisp 6-inch corn shells, each one loaded with slow-cooked carnitas,
> cheddar jack, salsa verde, shredded lettuce and pico de gallo. Six slices of
> fresh avocado across the three, plus fresh jalapeño on every one. The whole
> plate gets a drizzle of verde crema, our salsa verde folded into sour cream, so
> the heat and the cool land in the same bite. Pairs well with the Spicy Mary.
> Yes, at breakfast.

**Social:**

> Three tostadas. Half an avocado. Zero regrets.

## Notes

- Naming the verde crema as salsa verde folded into sour cream is the detail a
  food reporter will quote. It is in the press version and left out of the menu
  line only for length.
- The build spec is six avocado slices and twelve jalapeño slices across the
  three shells. "Half an avocado" in the social line is an existing claim from
  the campaign copy doc, not a measured figure, so it stays in social and out of
  anything a reporter might print.
- There is no egg in the dish despite the name. The "Yes, at breakfast" line
  leans into that rather than hiding it. An egg add-on would be an easy upsell
  if the kitchen wants one.
